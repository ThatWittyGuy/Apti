# Shared — ITSM / ITIL MCQs
### Applies to: JD4 (Collaboration Services), any JD mentioning IT operations, ticketing, support
> 35 Questions | 🟢 Easy | 🟡 Medium | 🔴 Hard

---

**Q1.** 🟢 What does ITSM stand for?
- A) Integrated Technology and Systems Management
- B) IT Service Management — the set of policies, processes, and practices for designing, delivering, managing, and improving IT services
- C) Information Technology and Security Management
- D) Integrated Ticketing and Support Module

**Answer: B**

---

**Q2.** 🟢 What does ITIL stand for?
- A) IT Infrastructure Library — a widely adopted framework of best practices for IT service management
- B) Integrated Technology and Innovation Lab
- C) IT Incident and Logging Library
- D) Information Technology Infrastructure Lifecycle

**Answer: A**

---

**Q3.** 🟢 What is an "incident" in ITIL?
- A) A planned change to an IT system
- B) An unplanned interruption to or reduction in the quality of an IT service
- C) A user request for a new software installation
- D) A scheduled system maintenance window

**Answer: B**

---

**Q4.** 🟢 What is a "service request" in ITIL?
- A) A report of an unplanned service outage
- B) A formal user request for something provided as a standard part of a service — e.g., password reset, software installation, access provisioning — following a predefined process
- C) A request from IT to the business for more budget
- D) An escalated incident requiring management attention

**Answer: B**

---

**Q5.** 🟢 What is an "SLA" (Service Level Agreement)?
- A) A software licensing agreement between vendor and customer
- B) A formal agreement defining the expected level of service — including response times, resolution times, availability targets, and responsibilities — between an IT service provider and its customers
- C) A security audit log agreement
- D) A salary agreement for IT service staff

**Answer: B**

---

**Q6.** 🟢 What is a "ticketing system" in IT support?
- A) A system for issuing physical access badges
- B) A software tool (e.g., ServiceNow, Jira Service Management, Zendesk) for logging, tracking, assigning, and resolving IT incidents and service requests with full audit trail
- C) A project management tool for IT teams
- D) A system for tracking software license tickets

**Answer: B**

---

**Q7.** 🟢 What is "IT service desk" vs. "IT help desk"?
- A) Service desk handles hardware; help desk handles software
- B) Help desk is a narrower, reactive break-fix concept; service desk is broader — handling incidents, service requests, communications, and acting as the single point of contact (SPOC) between users and IT per ITIL
- C) They are identical — the terms are fully interchangeable
- D) Service desk is for VIP users; help desk is for general users

**Answer: B**

---

**Q8.** 🟡 What is "problem management" in ITIL?
- A) Managing difficult users who frequently raise incidents
- B) The ITIL process focused on identifying and eliminating the root causes of recurring incidents — distinguishing between a "problem" (unknown cause) and a "known error" (identified cause with workaround/fix)
- C) Managing multiple incidents simultaneously
- D) A process for managing failed changes that cause incidents

**Answer: B**

---

**Q9.** 🟡 What is the difference between a "problem" and a "known error" in ITIL?
- A) Problems are hardware issues; known errors are software bugs
- B) A problem is the unknown underlying cause of one or more incidents; a known error is a problem where the root cause has been identified and a workaround or fix exists — recorded in the Known Error Database (KEDB)
- C) Known errors are resolved; problems are unresolved incidents
- D) They are the same concept with different names

**Answer: B**

---

**Q10.** 🟡 What is "change management" in ITIL?
- A) Managing organizational restructuring in IT
- B) The ITIL process controlling the lifecycle of all changes to IT infrastructure — ensuring changes are assessed for risk and impact, authorized, planned, implemented, and reviewed to minimize disruption
- C) Managing software version updates automatically
- D) A process for updating ITSM tool configurations

**Answer: B**

---

**Q11.** 🟡 What are the three types of changes in ITIL change management?
- A) Minor, Major, Critical
- B) Standard (pre-approved, low risk, routine), Normal (requires assessment and CAB approval), Emergency (urgent, fast-tracked with expedited approval due to critical impact)
- C) Planned, Unplanned, Emergency
- D) Hardware, Software, Network

**Answer: B**

---

**Q12.** 🟡 What is a "CAB" (Change Advisory Board)?
- A) A committee that approves the IT budget
- B) A group of stakeholders (IT, business, vendors) that reviews and advises on the approval of normal changes — assessing risk, impact, and scheduling before changes are authorized
- C) A board that manages IT vendor contracts
- D) A team responsible for testing changes before deployment

**Answer: B**

---

**Q13.** 🟡 What is "incident prioritization" based on in ITIL?
- A) The seniority of the user who reported the incident
- B) Impact (breadth of effect — how many users/services affected) × Urgency (time sensitivity — how quickly business is harmed if not resolved) — determining priority level and SLA response/resolution targets
- C) The time the incident was logged (first come, first served)
- D) The complexity of the technical issue

**Answer: B**

---

**Q14.** 🟡 What is "escalation" in ITSM — distinguish functional vs. hierarchical?
- A) Automatically increasing ticket priority after 24 hours
- B) Functional escalation transfers a ticket to a higher-skilled team (L1→L2→L3) when technical expertise is insufficient; hierarchical escalation notifies management when SLAs are at risk or business impact is high
- C) Escalating a ticket to the vendor for external support
- D) Moving a ticket from incident to problem management

**Answer: B**

---

**Q15.** 🟡 What is "mean time to resolve" (MTTR)?
- A) The mean time for a ticket to be reassigned
- B) The average time elapsed from when an incident is reported until it is fully resolved and closed — a key metric for measuring IT support efficiency
- C) Mean time between recurring incidents of the same type
- D) The maximum time allowed by the SLA to resolve an incident

**Answer: B**

---

**Q16.** 🟡 What is "mean time between failures" (MTBF)?
- A) The average time a support agent takes between two calls
- B) The average time a system operates between failures — a measure of reliability; higher MTBF means more reliable systems
- C) The time between an incident being reported and assigned
- D) The average time between scheduled maintenance windows

**Answer: B**

---

**Q17.** 🟡 What is a "configuration item" (CI) in ITIL?
- A) An IT configuration setting that requires change approval
- B) Any component (hardware, software, service, documentation) that needs to be managed to deliver an IT service — tracked in the Configuration Management Database (CMDB)
- C) A checklist item for system configuration audits
- D) A configuration template in an ITSM tool

**Answer: B**

---

**Q18.** 🟡 What is a "CMDB" (Configuration Management Database)?

- C) A centralized database for managing software licenses

**Q19.** 🟡 What is "service catalog management" in ITIL?

**Q20.** 🟡 What is "continual service improvement" (CSI) in ITIL?

**Q21.** 🟡 What is "knowledge management" in ITIL?

**Q22.** 🟡 What is "availability management" in ITIL?
