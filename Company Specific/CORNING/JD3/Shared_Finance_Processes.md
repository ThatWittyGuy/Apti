# Shared — Finance Business Processes MCQs
### Applies to: JD3 (SAP Finance Data), JD1 (ERP Transformation) — RTR, P2P, O2C, accounting concepts
> 35 Questions | 🟢 Easy | 🟡 Medium | 🔴 Hard

---

**Q1.** 🟢 What are the three main end-to-end finance processes in an ERP?
- A) Budget, Forecast, Report
- B) Procure-to-Pay (P2P), Order-to-Cash (O2C), and Record-to-Report (RTR)
- C) Plan, Execute, Close
- D) Invoice, Pay, Reconcile

**Answer: B**

---

**Q2.** 🟢 What is "Procure-to-Pay" (P2P)?
- A) The process from customer order to revenue receipt
- B) The end-to-end procurement cycle: identify need → purchase requisition → purchase order → goods receipt → vendor invoice → payment to vendor
- C) The process of paying employees
- D) Procuring cloud services and paying the subscription

**Answer: B**

---

**Q3.** 🟢 What is "Order-to-Cash" (O2C)?
- A) Ordering cash from the bank
- B) The end-to-end sales process: customer order → credit check → fulfillment → shipping → customer invoice → cash receipt → application to AR
- C) The process from purchase order to vendor payment
- D) Ordering raw materials to produce finished goods

**Answer: B**

---

**Q4.** 🟢 What is the "accounting equation"?
- A) Revenue − Expenses = Profit
- B) Assets = Liabilities + Equity — the foundational equation of double-entry accounting; every transaction must keep this balanced
- C) Debit = Credit × Tax Rate
- D) Income + Expenses = Net Assets

**Answer: B**

---

**Q5.** 🟢 What is a "balance sheet"?
- A) A report showing monthly sales vs. targets
- B) A financial statement showing a company's assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time — a snapshot of financial position
- C) A report balancing debits and credits in the GL
- D) A quarterly income and expense report

**Answer: B**

---

**Q6.** 🟢 What is an "income statement" (P&L)?
- A) A statement showing asset values over time
- B) A financial statement showing a company's revenues, expenses, and net profit or loss over a specific accounting period
- C) A statement of cash movements in and out of the company
- D) A report showing equity changes during the period

**Answer: B**

---

**Q7.** 🟢 What is a "purchase order" (PO)?
- A) An invoice sent by a vendor for goods delivered
- B) A formal document issued by a buyer to a vendor authorizing the purchase of specified goods or services at an agreed price and terms
- C) A request for goods from a warehouse
- D) A payment authorization for a vendor invoice

**Answer: B**

---

**Q8.** 🟢 What is "accounts payable" (AP) in plain finance terms?
- A) Money owed to the company by customers
- B) Money the company owes to vendors for goods and services received but not yet paid — a current liability on the balance sheet
- C) A company's cash held in bank accounts
- D) Payments due from employees

**Answer: B**

---

**Q9.** 🟡 What is "accounts receivable" (AR) and how does it appear on the balance sheet?
- A) Money owed to vendors; appears as a liability
- B) Money owed to the company by customers for goods/services delivered but not yet collected — a current asset on the balance sheet
- C) Revenue recognized before cash receipt; appears in equity
- D) Short-term loans receivable from banks

**Answer: B**

---

**Q10.** 🟡 What is "days payable outstanding" (DPO)?
- A) The number of days a purchase order has been open
- B) A metric measuring how long a company takes on average to pay its vendors: (Accounts Payable / Cost of Goods Sold) × Number of Days — higher DPO means the company holds cash longer
- C) The number of days until a vendor invoice is due
- D) The average number of days between PO and goods receipt

**Answer: B**

---

**Q11.** 🟡 What is "days sales outstanding" (DSO)?
- A) Days a sales order has been pending fulfillment
- B) A metric measuring how long it takes to collect payment after a sale: (Accounts Receivable / Revenue) × Number of Days — lower DSO means faster cash collection
- C) Days between customer order and invoice generation
- D) The average sales cycle length

**Answer: B**

---

**Q12.** 🟡 What is "working capital"?
- A) The capital invested in physical machinery
- B) Current Assets − Current Liabilities — a measure of a company's short-term liquidity and operational efficiency
- C) Total assets minus total liabilities
- D) Cash and equivalents held by the company

**Answer: B**

---

**Q13.** 🟡 What is "accrual accounting" vs. "cash accounting"?
- A) Accrual accounting records only cash transactions; cash accounting records all transactions
- B) Accrual accounting recognizes revenues and expenses when earned/incurred regardless of cash flow; cash accounting records transactions only when cash is received or paid
- C) They are the same — GAAP requires both simultaneously
- D) Cash accounting is for large companies; accrual is for small businesses

**Answer: B**

---

**Q14.** 🟡 What is "revenue recognition" in accounting?
- A) Recognizing a salesperson's achievement when a deal closes
- B) The accounting principle (ASC 606 / IFRS 15) determining when and how revenue is recorded — recognized when performance obligations are satisfied and control transfers to the customer, not necessarily when cash is received
- C) Identifying all revenue sources in a financial report
- D) Recognizing revenue by matching it to the tax year

**Answer: B**

---

**Q15.** 🟡 What is "trial balance" in accounting?
- A) A test run of the automated payment process
- B) A bookkeeping worksheet listing all G/L accounts with their debit or credit balances — used to verify that total debits equal total credits before preparing financial statements
- C) A balance sheet prepared midway through the fiscal year
- D) A bank statement reconciliation summary

**Answer: B**

---

**Q16.** 🟡 What is "depreciation" in plain finance terms?
- A) A decrease in the market value of company stock
- B) The systematic allocation of a fixed asset's cost over its useful life — spreading the expense across accounting periods rather than expensing the full cost upfront
- C) The write-off of uncollectible customer invoices
- D) A reduction in inventory value due to obsolescence

**Answer: B**

---

**Q17.** 🟡 What is "amortization"?
- A) Paying off a loan in installments
- B) The systematic expensing of an intangible asset (e.g., patent, software license, goodwill) over its useful life — similar to depreciation but for intangibles
- C) Accumulating interest on a savings account
- D) Converting foreign currency liabilities to local currency

**Answer: B**

---

**Q18.** 🟡 What is "goodwill" on a balance sheet?
- A) A measure of customer satisfaction
- B) An intangible asset arising when a company acquires another for more than the fair value of its net identifiable assets — representing brand, customer relationships, and synergies
- C) A liability for future warranty claims
- D) The market value of the company's intellectual property

**Answer: B**

---

**Q19.** 🟡 What is "cash flow statement" and its three sections?
- A) A report of cash budgets; three sections: income, expense, profit
- B) A financial statement showing cash inflows and outflows; three sections: Operating Activities (day-to-day business), Investing Activities (asset purchases/sales), Financing Activities (debt/equity)
- C) A bank statement summary; three sections: opening, transactions, closing
- D) A treasury report; three sections: receivables, payables, reserves

**Answer: B**

---

**Q20.** 🟡 What is "EBITDA"?
- A) Earnings Before Income Tax, Depreciation, and Amortization
- B) Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization — a common proxy for operating cash flow and business performance, removing financing and accounting effects
- C) Estimated Budgeted Income Tax and Depreciation Amount
- D) Effective Business Income Tax and Debt Adjustment

**Answer: B**

---

**Q21.** 🟡 What is "materiality" in financial reporting?
- A) The physical existence of assets being reported
- B) The accounting concept that an item is material if its omission or misstatement would influence the decisions of financial statement users — only material items require separate disclosure
- C) The requirement to report all physical assets
- D) A threshold for capital expenditure approval

**Answer: B**

---

**Q22.** 🟡 What is "IFRS" and why does it matter for global companies?
- A) International Finance Reporting Software — an ERP system
- B) International Financial Reporting Standards — a globally accepted set of accounting standards issued by IASB, used in 140+ countries; global companies like Corning must comply to enable consistent cross-border financial reporting
- C) Internal Finance and Risk Standards — internal corporate policies
- D) Integrated Financial Reporting System — a SAP reporting module

**Answer: B**

---

**Q23.** 🟡 What is "GAAP" and how does it differ from IFRS?
- A) General Accounting and Audit Practices — identical to IFRS
- B) Generally Accepted Accounting Principles — the US accounting standard (FASB); differs from IFRS in areas like inventory (LIFO allowed in GAAP, prohibited in IFRS), revenue recognition nuances, and lease accounting
- C) Global Audit and Accounting Principles — an international standard superseding IFRS
- D) Government Accounting and Audit Procedures — a public sector standard

**Answer: B**

---

**Q24.** 🟡 What is "internal controls" in a finance context?
- A) Controls that prevent employees from accessing the internet
- B) Policies, procedures, and mechanisms designed to ensure the accuracy and reliability of financial reporting, compliance with laws, and safeguarding of assets — e.g., segregation of duties, authorization limits, reconciliation requirements
- C) IT security controls within the finance department
- D) Controls on internal financial budgets

**Answer: B**

---

**Q25.** 🟡 What is "segregation of duties" (SoD)?
- A) Dividing the finance team into separate departments
- B) An internal control principle requiring that no single individual has control over all phases of a transaction (e.g., the person who creates a vendor cannot also approve and pay invoices) — reducing fraud and error risk
- C) A process for segregating confidential financial data
- D) Assigning different SAP roles to different system users

**Answer: B**

---

**Q26.** 🟡 What is a "purchase requisition" (PR) in P2P?
- A) A formal document sent to a vendor requesting a price quote
- B) An internal request document created by a department to trigger a purchasing process — approved internally before a purchase order is issued to a vendor
- C) A requisition from the purchasing team for budget approval
- D) A request to return goods to a vendor

**Answer: B**

---

**Q27.** 🟡 What is "goods receipt" (GR) in P2P?
- A) A customer's confirmation of receiving goods they ordered
- B) The SAP transaction recording that ordered goods have been physically received — triggering a debit to inventory/expense and a credit to the GR/IR clearing account
- C) A vendor's receipt confirming payment received
- D) A warehouse document listing available stock

---

**Q28.** 🔴 What is "hedge accounting" and when is it used?
- A) Accounting for investments in hedge funds
- B) A method of accounting where financial instruments (derivatives) used to hedge a risk (e.g., foreign currency, interest rate) are accounted for together with the hedged item — reducing P&L volatility from fair value changes; governed by IFRS 9 / ASC 815

**Q29.** 🔴 What is "transfer pricing" in a multinational corporation?
- A) The cost to transfer data between SAP systems
- B) The prices charged for transactions between related entities within the same corporate group (e.g., goods, services, IP) — subject to tax authority scrutiny to ensure arm's-length pricing and prevent profit shifting

**Answer: B**

---

**Q30.** 🔴 What is "consolidation" in group financial reporting?
- A) Combining multiple databases into one SAP system
- B) The process of combining the financial statements of a parent company and all its subsidiaries into a single set of group financial statements — eliminating intercompany transactions and presenting the group as a single economic entity
- C) Consolidating multiple bank accounts into one
- D) Merging cost centers within a single company code

**Q31.** 🔴 What is "CAPEX vs. OPEX"?
- A) Capital Expenses (one-time) vs. Operating Expenses (recurring) — but both appear on the income statement
- B) Capital Expenditure (CAPEX): spending on long-term assets (buildings, equipment, software) capitalized on the balance sheet and depreciated; Operating Expenditure (OPEX): day-to-day running costs expensed immediately on the P&L
- C) CAPEX is for IT; OPEX is for manufacturing
- D) CAPEX is approved by finance; OPEX is approved by department heads

**Answer: B**

---

**Q32.** 🔴 What is "FIFO vs. LIFO vs. weighted average" inventory costing?
- A) Three methods for ordering inventory from vendors
- B) Methods for determining the cost of goods sold and ending inventory: FIFO (oldest items sold first — lower COGS in rising prices); LIFO (newest items first — higher COGS, not allowed under IFRS); Weighted Average (average cost of all units)
- C) Three methods for physical inventory counting
- D) Shipping terms defining when ownership transfers

**Answer: B**

---

**Q33.** 🔴 What is "impairment" of an asset?
- A) Physical damage to a tangible asset
- B) A reduction in the carrying value of an asset on the balance sheet when its recoverable amount falls below its book value — recognized as a loss in the P&L; tested annually for goodwill under IFRS/GAAP
- C) The normal depreciation of a fixed asset
- D) A write-off of uncollectible receivables

**Answer: B**

---

**Q34.** 🔴 What is "deferred revenue"?
- A) Revenue that has been deferred (postponed) to next year for tax purposes
- B) A liability representing cash received from a customer before the performance obligation is fulfilled — recognized as revenue only when the service/product is delivered (e.g., annual software subscriptions)
- C) Revenue from deferred payment (installment) sales
- D) A provision for future revenue adjustments

**Answer: B**

**Q35.** 🔴 What is "earnings per share" (EPS) and why do public companies report it?
- A) The average salary per employee in the company
